# English papers

Load this file only for `mode=en_paper` or an English final audit.

## Information order

- Abstract: problem or object, method, quantitative result, and the conclusion supported by that result.
- Introduction: established knowledge, the unresolved gap, and the paper's response. List contributions only when they are distinct.
- Methods: object, setup, conditions, parameters, procedure, and rationale needed for reproduction.
- Results: observations first, interpretation second. State why a representative operating point was selected.
- Conclusion: answer the research question within the tested scope. Do not copy the abstract or results paragraph.

## Sentence decisions

- Put the technical subject and main verb where readers can find them. Keep passive voice when the procedure or object matters more than the actor.
- Prefer one stable term for one concept. Variation is useful only when the terms name different levels or components.
- Preserve qualifiers that define evidence, probability, scope, comparison, or measurement uncertainty.
- Replace empty metadiscourse with the actual result or relation. Keep signposting when it helps readers follow a long derivation or section.
- Break a long sentence only when it contains separable claims. Do not create short, repetitive sentences merely to vary rhythm.
- Use articles, number, tense, and comparison forms consistently; do not change a technical claim to solve a grammar problem.

## Evidence and style

Claims such as “widely used,” “the first,” “state of the art,” or “consistently” require direct evidence and an appropriate comparison set. A citation must remain next to the claim it supports.
